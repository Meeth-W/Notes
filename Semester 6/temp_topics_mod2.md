## Module 2 Topics: Traditional & Agile Software Development

- **Agile Development Methodology**: Understanding the core values of the Agile Manifesto, its principles, and designing an Agile development plan for startup environments.
- **Scrum Framework**: Deep dive into Scrum methodology including roles (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Development Team), ceremonies (Sprint Planning, Daily Standup, etc.), artifacts (Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog), and the benefits of continuous improvement.
- **Extreme Programming (XP)**: Core practices of XP (Pair Programming, Test-Driven Development, Continuous Integration) and how they enhance code quality and team collaboration.
- **Traditional vs. Agile Comparison**: Analyzing the advantages, disadvantages, and trade-offs of using Agile approaches compared to traditional plan-driven models (like Waterfall), especially for varying project environments (e.g., strict clients vs. flexible in-house projects).
- **Waterfall Model**: Detailed examination of the classical linear sequential approach, its distinct phases (Requirements, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment), and when it is appropriate to use.
- **Iterative and Incremental Development**: Understanding models that build software in repeating cycles or adding functionality in parts (Increments). Comparison with the Waterfall approach.
- **Evolutionary Process Models**:
  - **Prototyping Model**: Steps to build, test, and refine prototypes before full-scale development, handling unclear initial requirements, and evaluating trade-offs.
  - **Spiral Model**: Integrating risk management into iterative development, making it highly suitable for large, complex, and risk-intensive projects.
- **Specialized & Prescriptive Process Models**:
  - **Rapid Application Development (RAD)**: High-speed, component-based development principles and its scenarios for effectiveness.
  - **Component Assembly Model**: Utilizing reusable software components to improve efficiency and reduce development time.
  - **Concurrent Process Model**: Managing parallel activities and multiple states of development simultaneously within a software engineering context.
- **Software Process Selection**: Strategies for choosing the right development model based on project constraints, requirement stability, client characteristics, and risk factors.
