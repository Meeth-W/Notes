# Topics Covered in Module 4 (Design Engineering)

*   **Software Design Process**
    *   Stages of transition from requirements to software blueprints.
    *   Key phases: Architectural Design, Interface Design, Component-Level Design, and Data Design.
*   **User Interface (UI) Design**
    *   Anticipating user needs and improving User Experience (UX).
    *   Impact of poor UI on usability, cognitive load, and real-world failure cases (e.g., Hawaii missile alert).
*   **Data Design**
    *   Translating analysis models into data structures and database architectures.
    *   Its foundational role in influencing software architecture and performance.
*   **Component-Level Design & Refinement**
    *   Top-down strategy for breaking down abstract concepts into detailed specifications.
    *   Elaborating on internal algorithms, data structures, and procedural logic.
*   **Information Hiding & Encapsulation**
    *   Segregating design decisions to protect modules from extensive modifications.
    *   Restricting direct access to internal complexities to promote secure and maintainable boundaries.
*   **Design Model Elements**
    *   The Four Elements Design Model: Data, Architectural, Interface, and Component-Level design.
    *   Practical application of these elements in real-world systems (e.g., Online Shopping Systems).
*   **Modularity, Cohesion, and Coupling**
    *   Subdividing systems into manageable, independent units.
    *   Evaluating Functional Independence.
    *   Striving for High Cohesion (e.g., Sequential vs. Coincidental Cohesion) and Low Coupling (e.g., Data Coupling).
*   **Abstraction**
    *   Managing complexity by focusing on essential features (Procedural, Data, and Control Abstraction).
    *   The relationship and differences between Abstraction (bottom-up simplification) and Refinement (top-down elaboration).
*   **Software Design Principles**
    *   Core guidelines for robust systems: Single Responsibility Principle (SRP), Open/Closed Principle (OCP), Separation of Concerns (SoC), and DRY.
*   **Software Architecture & Control Hierarchy**
    *   Defining macro-level structures and lines of authority (program structure).
    *   Evaluating architectural patterns for scalability: Client-Server, Layered (N-Tier), and Microservices.
*   **Structural Partitioning & Procedure Design**
    *   Horizontal and Vertical (Factoring) partitioning to avoid monolithic "God modules."
    *   Micro-architecture planning involving optimal algorithms, data structures, and procedural flows for computational efficiency.
